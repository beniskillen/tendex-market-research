What to keep, what to drop, what to ask

This is a short window, not a Bloomberg seat.

Leo is right that an attention bet is different from a yes-or-no sports bet. He is also right that a reading screen is not the exchange. He is early on who pays, on the licence, and on the idea that Y Combinator or Jane Street comes next after one to five users. The thirty-day test should check the first point. It should not act out the second.

Keep.

  • The product. A score built from search, social posts and streams, which people can bet will rise or fall. That is what Forum publishes, and what Noise raised money for. Tendex’s page is the chart of that score. “Bloomberg for attention” is a weaker line. The site already uses the better one.
  • The split. The exchange takes the order. The screen keeps the trades, the overlay and the download. Oddpool shows this can exist. It also shows the exchange can close the gap by hiring the screen’s team.
  • The paper, named correctly. More Google searches have a published link to short-term stock prices, then a reversal. Cite Da, Engelberg and Gao, 2011. Do not cite the Federal Reserve.
  • Forum first, as a question. They are the Y Combinator company whose product matches the page. One conversation with the founders is the right next step. It is not a partnership yet.
  • A short unpaid trial with a cap. The 26 October note already says this. This file agrees. There is no paying customer that justifies open-ended co-founder hours.

Stop saying these out loud.

  • “A $53 billion attention market.” That July number is mostly the face value of sports bets. The first person who has read Pew will correct it.
  • “A $1.34 trillion attention economy.” That is advertising. Different buyer. Different product.
  • “Australian law does not apply because we are only a screen.” Not shown. Do not send Australians to Polymarket. The public record is that the regulator blocked it, and no prediction market is licensed here.
  • “One to five users, then Y Combinator, because Forum is in the batch.” Y Combinator’s standard deal is $500,000. $125,000 of that buys 7%. The rest is a note that matches the next round. It is not a rule that Y Combinator must fund the tools around its companies. Five people on fake money is a usability test.
  • “Jane Street and Jump are the first buyer.” No one has shown how they would use it, who would buy it, or that they have a budget. Jump sponsoring a student contest is not a software sale. Susquehanna trading on Kalshi is a different market.
  • “Nobody else built a terminal.” Nobody else put this score next to this price. Plenty of people built prediction screens. Say the second sentence.
  • “Equal equity” and “full-time after funding.” Ideas from the meeting. The numbers above do not support them, and the 26 October note does not accept them. Leave them out of anything sent to Forum.
  • The February contest and the roughly 80 Discord members as if they were this month’s traction. The contest is old. The member count was not checked.

The first move.

Do this first. Ben and Leo agree the hour cap, the stop date, and that Ben will not invoice anyone or promise users. Leo shows one live thing that is not the zero chart on the website: a real Polymarket attention market, the score it says it pays out on, and whether the trade list is saved or just a sample.

Then one question for Forum, not a pitch. What is stuck this month: finding the right practice users, getting people to come back, permission to use the data, or something a screen cannot fix? Ask what a useful first action is, who can say yes to a two-week test, and whether the score can be shown next to the price outside their app.

Who does what. Ben asks the question and writes it up. Leo is responsible for what the product really does. Forum gives the answer. Do not pay an influencer in this step.

What counts. A named person and a date for the next step, or a written no. Interest with no date is only interest.

Pass, hold, stop.

Pass

Forum names a problem Tendex can actually help with, and at least three traders who are not the founders try the chart without being paid. Talking about price is a separate pass. You do not need it in order to learn.

Hold

The chart is still a sample, Tendex is not allowed to use the score, or Forum is polite but vague. Do not fill the silence with a posting schedule.

Stop

Traders do not have this job, or the only ask is “bring us signups” with no way to track them and no pay. A paid growth service can be priced later, under its own name. Do not dress that up as a software company.

Day 90, only if the window is extended

One small group, with the full count: how many were reached, how many opened it, how many did the task, how many came back, and any invoice. Include the zeros. Still do not claim a market size.

Still not agreed.

Ben and Leo have not agreed a time cap. Leo has not confirmed the right to use the data. This file has not contacted Forum. Nobody has approved a slogan, a legal claim, or sending this research to Leo or Forum. The client page in Notion is still the working note. This report does not replace it, and it is not written back there unless Ben asks.
