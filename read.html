What to keep, what to drop, what to ask

The thesis is a window, not a seat at Bloomberg.

Leo is right that attention contracts are a different object from a sports yes/no, and right that a terminal is not an exchange. He is early on the buyer, the licence, and the idea that YC or a Jane Street desk is the next step after one to five users. The thirty-day test should buy evidence on the first point and refuse to perform the second.

Keep.

  • The object. A continuous series built from search, social and streams, traded long and short, is what Forum publishes and what Noise raised money for. Tendex’s page is the chart of that object. That positioning is cleaner than “Bloomberg for attention,” and it is the one the site already uses.
  • The split. Venue takes the order. Terminal keeps the tape, the overlay and the export. Oddpool is the existence proof, and also the warning that the split can close.
  • The paper, correctly named. Search attention has a published short-horizon link to retail stock prices, then a reversal. Cite Da, Engelberg and Gao, 2011. Do not cite the Federal Reserve.
  • Forum first, as a question. They are the YC company whose instrument matches the page. One founder conversation is the right next step. It is not a partnership.
  • A capped unpaid window. The 26 October note already says this. The market file agrees. There is no priced customer to justify open-ended co-founder hours.

Drop, or stop saying out loud.

  • “$53 billion attention market.” That July number is sports-heavy notional. Anyone who leads with it will be corrected by the first person who has read Pew.
  • “$1.34 trillion attention economy.” Advertising. Different buyer. Different product.
  • “We sidestep Australian law because we are a terminal.” Unshown. Do not recruit Australians into Polymarket while ACMA’s block and Moneysmart’s licence gap are the public record.
  • “One to five users, then YC, because Forum is in the batch.” YC’s standard deal is $500,000 for 7% plus an MFN SAFE. It is not a rule that YC must fund the tools around its companies. Five simulated users are a usability sample.
  • “Jane Street and Jump are the first buyer.” No workflow, no owner, no budget. Jump’s student contest is not a software sale. Susquehanna on Kalshi is a different market.
  • “Nobody else built a terminal.” Nobody else built this overlay for this index. Plenty of people built prediction terminals. Say the second sentence.
  • “Equal equity” and “full-time after funding.” Meeting proposals. They are not validated by any of the numbers above, and they are not accepted on the client page. Leave them out of the Forum brief entirely.
  • The February contest and the ~80 Discord members as if they were this month’s traction. Historical, or unverified.

The first move.

Action. Ben and Leo agree the cap, the stop date, and that Ben does not invoice anyone and does not promise users. Leo shows one live capability that is not the zeroed marketing chart: a real Polymarket attention market, the series it claims to settle on, and whether the tape is archived or illustrative.

Then one Forum question, not a pitch. What is the bottleneck this month: the right paper-traders, repeat use, a data permission, or something a terminal cannot touch? Ask what a useful action is, who can say yes to a two-week test, and whether the index series can be shown beside price outside their app.

Owner. Ben holds the question and the write-up. Leo holds the product truth. Forum holds the answer. No KOL is paid in this step.

Measure. A named owner and a dated next step, or a written no. Interest with no date is logged as interest.

Pass, hold, stop.

Pass

Forum names a bottleneck Tendex can touch, and at least three traders outside the founders attempt the overlay without being paid. A price conversation is a separate pass. It is not required to learn.

Hold

The chart is still a mock, the series is not licensable, or Forum is polite and unspecific. Do not fill the silence with a content calendar.

Stop

Traders do not have the job, or the only demand is “bring us signups” with no attribution and no pay. A growth-services offer can be priced later, under its own name. It should not be dressed up as a terminal company.

Day 90, only if the window is extended
