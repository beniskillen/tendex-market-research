Five layers. Two of them are the wrong market.

Do not size Tendex from ads or sports bets.

The market that matters is money someone might pay for software that shows an attention bet next to its score. It is not global advertising. It is not the face value of sports bets. Both of those numbers are large, current, and the wrong thing.

The bar lengths are only a guide. L1 and L2 are not the same kind of number as L4. L3 has no public real-money trading. L5 has no public revenue.

L1. The “attention economy” people quote.

WARC’s latest 2026 outlook, published 7–8 October 2026, expects companies to spend $1.34 trillion on ads in 2026, up 11.9%. Social ads are $394.6 billion of that. This is what advertisers pay websites and apps. Tendex does not sell an ad. Using this number as Tendex’s market would make every media company and every agency look like a customer. It explains why “attention” is a word with money behind it. It is not Tendex’s budget.

L2. The prediction-market boom people will paste in.

Pew Research Center, 23 September 2026, using data from The Block: the face value of bets on Kalshi and Polymarket rose from $26 billion in May 2026 to $53 billion in July. That count uses the one-dollar face of each contract, not the cash the trader paid. Sports was the biggest topic. An earlier Pew note, on 27 May 2026, said that since July 2024 sports was 80% of Kalshi and 39% of Polymarket. Sports, politics and crypto together were about 90% of each. In June and July 2026, during the World Cup, sports bets topped $58 billion on Kalshi and neared $22 billion on Polymarket across those two months.

Another site, Yes No Odds, read Pew’s table and got about 79% sports in July, and about 88% of the May-to-July rise. That is their sum, not Pew’s sentence. It is labelled as worked out. Pew’s own point is enough: the boom is sports bets.

On 7 October 2025, the owner of the New York Stock Exchange, ICE, filed that it would invest up to $2 billion in Polymarket and sell Polymarket’s event data to big investors as a mood signal. ICE’s own release put the value at about $8 billion before the investment. That is real interest from a big institution in event odds. It is the strongest demand proof nearby. It is not an order for an attention-score screen.

Three sizes, and no fake ceiling.

The whole market. Money people and trading teams might spend on software that puts an attention bet’s price next to its score. Nobody has counted those buyers. Do not use the ad number or the sports-bet number instead.

The part Tendex could reach, as a sketch only. People who pay for themselves, at Oddpool’s prices, in a country the exchange is allowed to serve. The sum is: people you can reach, times the share who need this every month, times the price. Both the headcount and the share are unknown. At $30 to $100 a month, 100 paying accounts is $36,000 to $120,000 a year. 1,000 accounts is $0.36 million to $1.2 million. That is arithmetic on a price range. It is not a forecast. The 100 and the 1,000 are stand-ins, not research. They are C4 and C5 in the ledger.

What Tendex can count today. Public revenue is zero. Invite list, no card, no charge. If Forum later pays for a small test, that is services money, and only if there is an invoice. It is still not revenue from the screen.

What would make a number safe to use.

  • A count of live attention bets, by exchange, using real money rather than points.
  • Oddpool’s last subscriber count, or Kalshi’s plan for that product, from the team rather than the pricing page.
  • A price test: show ten people the trade list, ask what they pay now, and whether they would pay $30.
  • Forum’s own count of people active each week, and whether any of them asked for a chart outside the app.
Worked figures are C1–C8 in the ledger.
