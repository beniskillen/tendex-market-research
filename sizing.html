Five layers, two of them refused

Do not size the terminal off the billboard or the sportsbook.

TAM here is the money a buyer might spend on software and data for attention contracts. It is not global advertising. It is not the face value of sports contracts. Both of those numbers are large, current, and the wrong object.

Bar widths are a reading aid. L1 and L2 are not comparable to L4. L3 has no public real-money volume. L5 has no public revenue.

L1. The attention economy people quote.

WARC’s Q3 2026 outlook, published 7–8 October 2026, forecasts global ad spend of $1.34 trillion in 2026, up 11.9%. Social is $394.6 billion of that. This measures what advertisers pay publishers and platforms. Tendex does not sell an impression. Using this number as TAM would make every media company and every agency look like a customer. It is context for why “attention” is a word with a budget behind it. It is not the budget.

L2. The prediction-market boom people will paste in.

Pew Research Center, 23 September 2026, reading The Block: combined monthly global notional on Kalshi and Polymarket rose from $26 billion in May 2026 to $53 billion in July. The measure is taker notional at a one-dollar contract face, not the cash the trader paid. Sports was the largest topic. A 27 May 2026 Pew note, covering volume since July 2024, put sports at 80% of Kalshi and 39% of Polymarket, with sports, politics and crypto together about 90% of each book. June and July 2026 sports notional topped $58 billion on Kalshi and neared $22 billion on Polymarket across those two months, coinciding with the World Cup.

A secondary reading of Pew’s category table, by Yes No Odds, puts sports at about 79% of July’s combined notional and about 88% of the May-to-July increase. That arithmetic is labelled derived. It is not needed to make the point. The point is Pew’s: the boom is sports contracts.

ICE’s 8-K of 7 October 2025 says the company agreed to invest up to $2 billion in Polymarket and to distribute Polymarket’s event-driven data as sentiment indicators. The company release puts the valuation at about $8 billion pre-investment. That is institutional interest in event probabilities. It is the strongest demand evidence in the neighbourhood. It is not a purchase order for an attention-index terminal.

TAM, SAM, SOM, without a fake ceiling.

TAM. Software and data spend by people and desks who need an attention contract’s price beside its settlement series. No census of those buyers exists. Do not substitute L1 or L2.

SAM, sketch only. Self-serve prosumers at the Oddpool band, in a geography the venue can actually serve. Formula, not a result: reachable accounts × share who have a recurring job × price. Both the account count and the share are unknown. At $30 to $100 a month, 100 paying accounts is $36,000 to $120,000 a year. 1,000 accounts is $0.36 million to $1.2 million. Those are arithmetic on a price band. They are not a forecast. The 100 and the 1,000 are placeholders, the same way a 0.3 penetration rate would be. C4 and C5 in the ledger.

SOM. The only operable figure is zero public Tendex revenue. Invite list, unpaid, no card. A Forum pilot fee would be services revenue if it is invoiced. It would still not be terminal revenue.

What would make a number legal to use.

  • A count of live attention contracts, by venue, with real capital rather than a points balance.
  • Oddpool’s last subscriber count, or Kalshi’s plan for that product, from the team rather than the pricing page.
  • A price test: ten people shown the tape, asked what they pay now, and whether they would pay $30.
  • Forum’s own number for weekly actives, and whether any of them asked for an outside chart.
Worked figures are C1–C8 in the ledger.
