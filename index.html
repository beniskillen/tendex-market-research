Attention terminal inside a prediction-market boom

The volume is not the market.

Tendex wants to line a contract’s price up with the attention series it settles on. That job is specific. The $53 billion July figure next to it is not. It is notional sports turnover on Kalshi and Polymarket, counted at a one-dollar face value, not cash paid for cultural attention and not money anyone has paid for a terminal.

The decision this file is for

What in Leo’s picture survives contact with the tape.

The 20 October meeting asked Ben to help approach Forum and to consider a growth co-founder seat. The 26 October note on the client page narrowed that to a capped, unpaid, thirty-day evidence window. Neither note is an accepted agreement. This file tests the market story those notes depend on.

The category is named by other people.

Forum, a YC Winter 2026 company, publishes an Attention Index from search, social and streaming, and says trading is still simulated. Noise raised a Paradigm-led seed to trade relevance, then rewrote the product. Attention-as-a-contract is not only Leo’s phrase.

The terminal job is narrower than the boom.

A Bloomberg-shaped seat is the wrong price anchor. The observed self-serve band for prediction-market data is Oddpool’s $0, $30 and $100 plans. Those plans are closed to new subscribers because the company is joining Kalshi.

Search attention and stock prices are a real paper.

Da, Engelberg and Gao, Journal of Finance, 2011. Google search volume led retail-heavy stocks for about two weeks, then reversed inside a year. It is not a Federal Reserve paper, and it is not a licence to trade a cultural index.

The exchange can swallow the terminal.

Oddpool is the closest finished version of “be the data layer, not the venue.” YC now marks it inactive. Zora ships its own research agent. A Forum pilot is a hypothesis about a bottleneck, not proof the venue will rent the view forever.

Where Tendex sits

Upper right. Continuous, and not the venue.

Left is a yes-or-no event contract. Right is a continuous attention series. Bottom is the place the contract trades. Top is software that reads it. Tendex’s public page claims the top right: price and the oracle, across venues, with a tape. Forum and Noise claim the bottom right. Kalshi, Polymarket and the sportsbooks claim the bottom left, which is where the volume actually is.

Four buyers, only one is in range

Count the job before the message.

Someone already trading attention.

They need the price and the settlement series on one axis, plus a tape. This is the only job the public Tendex page actually demonstrates. The count of those people is unpublished.

A prediction-market desk that wants one schema.

Oddpool, Polysights, Hashdive, Stand and Betmoar already sell pieces of this. Attention is a thin slice of their book. A second terminal has to win a workflow, not a category slogan.

A venue that wants distribution.

Forum might pay for qualified paper-traders, or later for real accounts, if it has a bottleneck and a budget. That is a services contract. It does not prove a subscription.

A brand that wants to hedge culture.

Forum’s own sentence. No named repeat hedger was found. Attention and sales do not have to move together. Leave this buyer off the first pitch.
