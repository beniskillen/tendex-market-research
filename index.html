A reading screen, next to a boom that is mostly sports

The big number is not Tendex’s market.

Tendex wants one simple view: the price of a bet, next to the attention score that bet pays out on. That is a small job. The $53 billion July number beside it is a different thing. It is the face value of sports bets on Kalshi and Polymarket, not cash anyone paid, and not money anyone has paid for a screen like Tendex.

What this file is for

What in Leo’s picture still holds.

On 20 October, Leo asked Ben to help approach Forum and to think about joining as the growth co-founder. The 26 October note cut that back to a short unpaid trial, with a time cap, to gather proof. Neither note is a signed deal. This file checks the market story those notes rely on.

Other people already use this idea.

Forum, a Y Combinator company from winter 2026, builds an attention score from search, social posts and streams. It says trading is still with fake money. Noise raised money from Paradigm to trade what is popular, then changed the product. This is not only Leo’s phrase.

The screen is a smaller business than the boom.

A Bloomberg-priced seat is the wrong comparison. The only posted prices for this kind of data are Oddpool’s free, $30 and $100 plans. Those plans are closed to new buyers because Oddpool is joining Kalshi.

The stock-price paper is real. The citation is not.

Da, Engelberg and Gao, Journal of Finance, 2011. More Google searches came before higher prices in small stocks for about two weeks. Prices then fell back within a year. This is not a Federal Reserve paper, and it is not permission to trade a culture score.

The exchange can take the screen in-house.

Oddpool is the closest finished version of “we read the market, we do not run it.” Y Combinator now lists it as inactive. Zora built its own research tool. A test with Forum is a question about their problem. It is not proof they will rent Tendex forever.

Where Tendex sits

Top right. A running score, not the exchange.

Left means a yes-or-no bet on an event. Right means a score that keeps moving with attention. The bottom is where the bet trades. The top is software that reads it. Tendex’s public page claims the top right: price next to the score, across exchanges, with the list of trades. Forum and Noise claim the bottom right. Kalshi, Polymarket and the sportsbooks sit at the bottom left. That is where the trading actually is.

Four possible buyers. Only one is close.

Name the job before the message.

Someone already trading attention.

They want the price and the score on one chart, plus the list of trades. This is the only job the public Tendex page actually shows. Nobody has published how many of these people exist.

A prediction trader who wants one clean feed.

Oddpool, Polysights, Hashdive, Stand and Betmoar already sell pieces of this. Attention is a small part of their markets. A second screen has to be more useful, not just a new slogan.

An exchange that wants more of the right users.

Forum might pay for the right practice users, or later for real accounts, if it has a clear problem and a budget. That would be a services job. It would not prove people will subscribe to Tendex.

A brand that wants to insure against a trend dying.

Forum says this is a use. No repeat customer of that kind was found. Attention and sales do not have to move together. Leave this buyer out of the first conversation.
