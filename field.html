Exchanges, screens, and the free chart

Crowded where the trading is. Thin where Tendex says it stands.

Fifteen active or recently active players. Eight get a full reading because they sit on the decision: Forum, Noise, Oddpool, Polymarket, Kalshi, Polysights, Zora, and the free charts. The rest are named so “nobody has built this” can be checked, not trusted.

Eight readings.

Forum — the partner, and an exchange

Y Combinator, winter 2026. Owen Botkin, who the Y Combinator profile says used to trade shares long and short at Balyasny. Joseph Thomas, who the same profile says wrote software at GEICO, NASA and DreamwaveAI. Those job histories were not checked with the employers. The product is a score and an exchange. Trading on the public profile uses fake money. The docs say a regulated US market is the plan, and the licence is still pending. That is weaker than “we have applied and we are the first licensed attention exchange.” Y Combinator partner listed: Jared Friedman. Place: New York. Team size on the profile: 2.

Noise — the funded alternative

Paradigm led a $7.1 million early round announced on 14 January 2026. The Block, quoting the founder, adds a private test with 1,300 users across 14 markets, using social data from Kaito, and a public launch on Base that was then “coming months” away. Noise’s own recap adds the claim that 62% were still active and that sessions lasted 17 minutes. An editor’s note says that post describes an older product. Today’s line is that you buy the trends you believe in. Compare today’s product. Do not describe the January design to Forum.

Oddpool — the ghost of the plan

Y Combinator, spring 2026, now listed as inactive. Public prices: $0, $30 and $100, plus a custom archive of the Kalshi and Polymarket order books for big customers. The pricing page says new sign-ups are closed. Avi Arora’s LinkedIn post says they raised $3 million and are joining Kalshi. The raise is the founder’s post, not a filing opened here. The point does not need the $3 million: the best outside data product for the two big exchanges chose to go inside one of them.

Polymarket — culture is a label, not a moving score

People bet against each other on whether an event happens. On 7 October 2025, ICE filed that it would invest up to $2 billion and sell the data to big investors as a mood signal. The release also names culture next to markets, politics and sport. Culture is a tag, not a score that keeps moving. Other tools already sit on top: Betmoar, Stand, Polysights, Hashdive and a long list. A January 2026 article put Betmoar near $300 million of trading through the tool and 1,262 users. Treat that as press, not an audit.

Kalshi — where the sports bets cleared

The regulated US place for event bets. Pew’s split says sports has been most of its trading. It is the wrong first customer for an attention chart, and the right reminder that “prediction markets” in a pitch usually means this. Oddpool joining Kalshi pulls that comparison onto the sports-and-events side of the map.

Polysights — the crowded screen

It sells itself as one screen for live odds, orders, wallet profit, a tool that flags unusual wallets, a sports desk, and a data feed. Hashdive, also described in the press as Unusual Predictions, sells a trader score and a filter. Stand adds Kalshi and Opinion to one ticket. None of these puts an attention score next to the price. All of them are what a trader opens before they open Tendex. The gap is not “no screen exists.” The gap is “none of them pay out on a culture score.”

Zora — the exchange that built the tool

Content markets with their own charts, a command line and a research helper. Proof that an exchange can ship the research view and keep the user. Not proof of Tendex’s price. Use it as the downside in the Forum meeting: “if you build this chart, our screen is just a feature.”

Free substitutes — Trends, and the venue’s own page

Google Trends is free, and it is the tool behind the 2011 paper. Google can revise it, it is delayed, and it is not a bet. Forum’s own app is the other free option for a Forum trader. Tendex only earns a fee when someone needs the score and the trade list together, across exchanges, badly enough to leave the free tab.

Named, not fully checked.

ATTN Markets describes trading on a person’s brand attention, and says it has not launched. FRNT talks about attention and being seen. Kaito sells a crypto “who is being talked about” score and was a data source for Noise. A 17 October 2026 third-party price check lists TradingView at $14.95, $34.95 and $69.95 a month. Lucid Terminal lists its own seat from free to $700 a month. It is a futures screen, not this market. Prediction Hunt compares exchanges. Kairos has published a negotiated fee. None of these checkouts were opened again for a fresh price on 27 October, except Koyfin, Oddpool and the Unusual Whales starting price. Do not invent the missing prices.

What Tendex has actually put on the public site.

tendexterminal.com, read 27 October 2026. A light screen. Price in blue, attention in amber. The sentence on the page: data for attention markets, each market’s price laid over the attention score it pays out on, across exchanges, with a saved order book and trade list. Early access, no card. A note that it is not an offer to trade. The main chart is a sample: price 0.00, attention 0.0, and three example trades. Polymarket is the only exchange named there. Forum and Kalshi are not on the public page. That matches the meeting’s “Polymarket first.” It contradicts any claim that a multi-exchange screen is already live.

Company pages beat trade press. Trade press beats the meeting.
