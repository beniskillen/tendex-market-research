Venues, terminals, and the free chart

The field is busy where the volume is, and thin where Tendex claims to stand.

Fifteen active or recently active players. Eight of them get a full reading because they sit on the decision: Forum, Noise, Oddpool, Polymarket, Kalshi, Polysights, Zora, and the free substitutes. The rest are named so the “nobody has built this” line can be checked rather than trusted.

Eight readings.

Forum — the partner, and a venue

YC Winter 2026. Owen Botkin, previously long/short equities at Balyasny according to the YC profile. Joseph Thomas, software at GEICO, NASA and DreamwaveAI, same source. Those biographies were not re-checked with the employers. The product is an index and an exchange. Trading on the public profile is simulated. The docs say a US regulated market is the plan, pending DCM/DCO designation. That is weaker than “we have applied and are the first licensed attention exchange.” Primary partner listed: Jared Friedman. Location: New York. Team size on the profile: 2.

Noise — the funded alternative

Paradigm led a $7.1 million seed announced 14 January 2026. The Block’s account, from the founder, adds a 1,300-user invite testnet across 14 markets using Kaito social data, and a Base mainnet that was then “coming months” away. Noise’s own recap adds the 62% retention and 17-minute session claims, then an editor’s note: the post describes an older product. Today’s line is that you buy the trends you believe in. Compare the current mechanic. Do not brief Forum with the January design.

Oddpool — the ghost of the plan

YC Spring 2026, now inactive. Public pricing: $0, $30, $100, plus a negotiated enterprise archive of the Kalshi and Polymarket book. The pricing page says new subscriptions are closed. Avi Arora’s LinkedIn post says they raised a $3 million seed and are joining Kalshi. The raise is a founder post, not a filing opened here. The strategic fact does not need the $3 million: the best outside data layer for the two big venues chose to go inside one of them.

Polymarket — the cultural book that is still an event book

Peer-to-peer event contracts. ICE, via an 8-K on 7 October 2025, agreed to invest up to $2 billion and to distribute the data to institutions as sentiment. The release also names culture alongside markets, politics and sport. Culture is a tag, not a continuous index. Builders already sit on top: Betmoar, Stand, Polysights, Hashdive and a long tail. A January 2026 trade piece put Betmoar near $300 million of builder volume and 1,262 users. Treat that as press, not an audit.

Kalshi — where the sports notional cleared

The regulated US event-contract venue. Pew’s category split says sports has been most of its volume. It is the wrong first customer for an attention overlay, and the right reminder that “prediction markets” in a pitch deck usually means this book. Oddpool joining it pulls the terminal analogue onto the sports-and-events side of the map.

Polysights — the crowded terminal

Markets itself as one terminal for live odds, order flow, wallet P&L, an insider finder, a sports desk and an API. Hashdive (also described in press as Unusual Predictions) sells a smart-score and a screener. Stand adds Kalshi and Opinion to a ticket. None of these is an attention-oracle overlay. All of them are what a trader opens before they open Tendex. The flank is not “no terminal exists.” The flank is “none of them settle a cultural index.”

Zora — the venue that built the tool

Content markets with their own analytics, a command line and a research agent. Proof that a venue can ship the research view and keep the user. Not proof of Tendex’s price. Use it as the downside case in the Forum meeting: “if you build this chart, our terminal is a feature.”

Free substitutes — Trends, and the venue’s own page

Google Trends is free and is the academic instrument behind the 2011 paper. It is revisable, delayed, and not a contract. Forum’s own app is the other substitute for a Forum trader. Tendex earns a place only when the user needs the series and the tape together, across venues, badly enough to leave the free tab.

Named, not torn down.

ATTN Markets describes pre-launch trading on personal-brand attention. FRNT talks about attention and competitive visibility. Kaito sells crypto mindshare and was a Noise data source. TradingView, from a 17 October 2026 third-party price check, lists Essential at $14.95, Plus at $34.95 and Premium at $69.95 a month. Lucid Terminal publishes its own seat from free to $700 a month and is a futures terminal, not this category. Prediction Hunt compares venues. Kairos has published a negotiated platform-fee model. None of these checkouts were re-opened for a fresh price on 27 October except Koyfin, Oddpool and the Unusual Whales floor. Do not invent the missing prices.

What Tendex has actually shipped in public.

tendexterminal.com, read 27 October 2026. IBM Plex, a light terminal, price in blue and attention in amber. The sentence on the page: institutional data for attention markets, each market’s price overlaid with the attention oracle it settles on, across exchanges, with an archived book and tape. Early access, no card. A disclaimer that it is not an offer to trade. The hero chart is a mock: price 0.00, attention 0.0, and a three-row sample tape. Polymarket is the only venue name on the hero. Forum and Kalshi are not on the public page. That matches the meeting’s “Polymarket first” and contradicts any outside claim that the multi-venue terminal is already live.

Company pages beat trade press. Trade press beats the meeting.
